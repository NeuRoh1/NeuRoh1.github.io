Our research

Flexible enough to learn.
Stable enough to last.

The Roh Lab studies how molecular and circuit mechanisms enable the brain to adapt to experience while maintaining reliable function. We connect basic discovery with questions about brain health and disease.

Plasticity

Experience shapes
the circuit.

How does experience change the connections that support behavior?

We investigate synaptic protein dynamics and interactions between excitatory and inhibitory neurons to understand how cortical circuits reorganize during plasticity.

Sleep & refinement

The brain changes
at rest.

How do sleep and circadian rhythms shape circuit maintenance?

We examine how sleep and wake states relate to molecular dynamics, neural activity, and the preservation or reorganization of circuit connections.

Resilience

Protecting the capacity
to adapt.

Why do some circuits remain functional while others become vulnerable?

We study mechanisms of circuit stability and dysfunction, with the goal of identifying ways to preserve or restore function in neurodegenerative disease.

From fundamental mechanisms
to brain health.

Our research seeks principles that explain how neural circuits remain both flexible and stable. Synaptic molecules such as neuronal pentraxin 2 (NPTX2) provide an entry point for connecting molecular regulation with circuit organization and behavior.

Our translational goal is to identify mechanisms that could protect circuit function and cognition in conditions such as Alzheimer’s disease. We investigate these possibilities through experiments that connect molecular changes, cellular activity, network function, and behavior.

Our approach

One question.
Multiple scales.

We combine targeted perturbations with measurements of living circuits, then connect those changes to sleep, behavior, and cognition.

Molecular & genetic manipulation

Test how specific molecular processes contribute to circuit function.

Two-photon calcium imaging

Observe cellular activity within living neural circuits.

Surface-protein imaging

Track the dynamics of proteins at the neuronal surface.

Silicon-probe electrophysiology

Measure neuronal firing and coordinated network activity.

Behavioral assessment

Relate circuit changes to behavior, including pain and motor function.

Sleep & circadian analysis

Connect EEG-defined sleep states and daily rhythms with brain function.
